IN EVERYDAY LIFE · 02 · SPATIAL SUMMARY

FIVE CHECKS OF THE MAP

The complaint map is only a starting point. These five checks compare it with restaurant activity, income, season and inspection results.
They show associations in the available records, not a single explanation for why rats appear where they do.

01 · RESTAURANT DENSITY

Restaurants and Complaints by ZIP Code

We compared restaurant permits with outdoor rat complaints after adjusting both for land area.

Each point represents one ZIP Code. Using density rather than raw totals makes compact and large ZIP Codes easier to compare.

RESULTr = —

ZIP Codes with more restaurants per square kilometre also tend to record more complaints per square kilometre. This is an association, not evidence that restaurants cause the complaints.

02 · INCOME & UNEQUAL EXPOSURE

Complaint Rates Do Not Match Inspection Results

We grouped ZIP Codes by the share of residents with low or moderate incomes.

Bars show 311 complaints per 10,000 residents. Dots show the percentage of completed inspections that recorded rat activity.

RESULT—%

The group with the highest low/moderate-income share does not submit the most complaints, but it has the highest positive-inspection rate. Complaint totals alone therefore miss part of the pattern.

03 · DIFFERENT URBAN CONDITIONS

Not Every Complaint Hotspot Looks the Same

A median split places each ZIP Code into one of four complaint-and-restaurant groups.

This simple classification separates places where both densities are high from high-complaint ZIP Codes with relatively few restaurants. Those places are likely shaped by other conditions not tested here.

04 · SEASONALITY

Complaints Peak in Midsummer

Monthly averages are calculated from complete years between 2020 and 2025.

We use the date each outdoor rat complaint was created and leave out the incomplete current year, which would pull later months downward.

PEAK MONTH—

Reports rise through spring, reach their highest monthly average in summer and then fall toward winter. The map will look different depending on which part of the year is sampled.

05 · COMPLAINTS VS. CONFIRMED ACTIVITY

Complaints and Inspections Usually Move Together

We compared complaint rates with the share of NYC Health inspections marked “Failed for Rat Activity.”

The comparison includes ZIP Codes with at least 100 completed inspection outcomes, so very small samples do not dominate the chart.

RANK CORRELATIONρ = —

ZIP Codes with higher complaint rates generally have more inspections that confirm rat activity. The points do not line up perfectly, which is why complaints and inspections should be read together.
