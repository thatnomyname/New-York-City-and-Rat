Rat activity was recorded in 54% of follow-up inspections completed in 2025. We next looked at a different question: did the number of nearby 311 reports change after a government baiting visit?

The two datasets cannot be linked case by case. A baiting visit and a public report may occur days or weeks apart, and they do not share a complaint identifier. For each baiting event, we counted reports within 250 metres during the 28 days before and the 28 days after, excluding the event day.

An event was included only when both 28-day periods were covered and no other baiting date was recorded within 250 metres. Where several eligible events fell in the same 250-metre grid cell, we kept the earliest one to avoid repeatedly sampling the same area.

Each event was paired with one comparison location in the same borough, between 500 metres and 5 kilometres away. The pair had similar earlier report counts and short-term trends, and the comparison location had no baiting record within 250 metres during the observation period. This produced 650 matched pairs.

For every pair, we calculated the before-to-after change at the baited location and subtracted the change at its comparison location. The cards report the average counts at baited locations and the average difference between the two groups.

We expected reports to fall after baiting. In these matched locations, they did not: reports increased relative to the comparison locations.

This result does not mean that baiting produced more rats. It may reflect which places receive treatment, which sightings residents choose to report, or difficult conditions that remain after a visit.

Baiting is not placed randomly across the city. Locations with persistent problems may receive repeated visits, so a baited site may begin with a harder-to-control rat problem than a comparison site with a similar recent reporting trend.

NYC Health · Inspection process ↗

The records measure different things. A 311 report requires someone to see a rat and submit a complaint; an inspection records what staff found during a visit. Neither dataset directly measures the size of the rat population, and the records do not necessarily refer to the same incident.

NYC Health · Rat complaints and NYC’s rat problem ↗

The data also leave out some relevant changes, including private pest control, repairs by property owners and shifts in food waste or building conditions. For that reason, this comparison describes a pattern in the records rather than the causal effect of baiting.
